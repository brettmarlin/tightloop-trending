# How the 48-hour reading list is picked

This is the selection criteria for the 48-hour reading list posted in #whats-trending in the Tightloop Discord. If you think the picks should change, open a pull request against this file. See "Proposing a change" at the bottom.

## What it is

Every two days, five short items on what senior product people building with AI should actually be paying attention to. Each item is one theme, backed by one to three links. The goal is signal, not volume.

## The window

- Only things published or meaningfully discussed in the last 48 hours count.
- An older piece can come back in only if it's at the center of a conversation happening inside the window (for example, it hit the top of Hacker News today).
- If a theme ran in the previous list, it only returns with something genuinely new.

## Signals

These are the vectors that make something count as trending. Each one is its own section so you can propose changing just that one: add a signal, drop one, or argue it should count for more or less.

### Signal: Convergence
Several independent sources make the same point inside the window. This is the main thing that makes something trending, and themes rank higher the more independent sources land on them.

### Signal: Practitioner conversation
Builders, designers and product leaders are actively discussing it (public X posts and Hacker News threads; Reddit and other communities are a goal, not a live source yet), not just sharing it.

### Signal: Primary source
There's an original study, launch, changelog or first-hand post behind it, not only coverage of one.

### Signal: Changes how we build
It shifts how a product, design or engineering team works next week: a new capability, tool, workflow or hard data point.

### Signal: Trusted curators
It's picked up by newsletters and writers this group already reads (for example Lenny's Newsletter, Nielsen Norman Group, Jakob Nielsen's UX Roundup, Nate's Newsletter, The Neuron, TLDR).

### Signal: Industry moves
Funding, launches, pricing or policy from established outlets, counted only when it changes what product people do next.

Picks start from a fixed set of followed sources (X accounts, Substacks, newsletters and Hacker News), plus whatever those sources point to. Want a source added or dropped? Propose it.

## Topics

Items should land in or next to one of these topic areas. Each is its own section so you can propose adding, dropping or reshaping one.

### Topic: Roles and ownership
How AI is changing the product, design and engineering roles, and who owns what.

### Topic: AI product and agent UX
Interfaces, context, trust, review and verification.

### Topic: Building with AI
New tools, workflows and capabilities that change how product gets made.

### Topic: Quality, taste and judgment
What good means when output is cheap.

### Topic: Running AI in practice
Cost, adoption, org design and governance inside real teams and companies.

## Quality bar

An item makes the list only if it passes all of these:

1. **Useful to a senior product person.** It changes how someone here would think, decide or build. Interesting but irrelevant doesn't count.
2. **Has substance.** Real data, a real launch, a working example or a clearly argued position. Not just a hot take.
3. **Primary where possible.** Link the original study, post or announcement before coverage of it.
4. **Holds up.** Claims are checked against the source. Numbers are quoted as the source states them. Every link is opened before it posts, and a link that's dead or can't be checked is dropped.
5. **Says something in one sentence.** If the point can't be stated plainly, it isn't ready.

When two items make the same point, they're merged into one theme with both links.

## Excluded

- Hype, launch fluff and "AI will change everything" pieces with nothing concrete in them.
- Engagement bait, threads written to farm replies and listicles.
- Paywalled links when a free primary source exists.
- Rumors and unverified leaks.
- Pure politics, culture war and drama, unless it directly changes how product gets built.
- Self-promotion by members of this server, or by whoever curates the list. Share your own work in the other channels.
- Anything about a private person, private company details or private conversations.
- Repeats of a recent list without new information.

## Format

- Header: `**48-hour reading list** (<dates>)`.
- Five numbered items. Each has a bold headline that states the point as a short claim, a summary of one or two plain sentences, then one to three links.
- Links are written as `[short label](<url>)` so Discord doesn't expand previews, separated by ` · `.
- Plain language, plain punctuation, no em dashes and no AI-sounding copy.
- Split into Discord messages of under 1,900 characters each, posted in order.
- Every list ends with a link back to this file.

## Proposing a change

Anyone in Tightloop can propose a change:

1. Open a pull request that edits this file. Keep each PR to one idea and one section: a signal to add, drop or reweight, a topic to add or reshape, or a rule to tighten.
2. In the description, say what you'd change and why, ideally with an example item that would have made or missed a recent list.
3. Brett reviews and merges. Merged changes apply starting with the next list.

If you'd rather not open a PR, open an issue or just say it in #whats-trending.
